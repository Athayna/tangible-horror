\subsection{Procedure}
\label{sec:study-procedure}

Sessions took place in a work room at Hochschule RheinMain that we prepared as the play area, with the proxy stations set up and calibrated before participants arrived. We told participants beforehand that they would take part in a study on VR horror, and they gave verbal consent; we used no written consent forms, a point we return to in the limitations. After a short briefing on the headset, the controls, and the horror content (participants knew the genre, not the individual challenges), each participant put on the Quest~3 and played their first condition until they escaped the cabin. They then took the headset off and filled in the IPQ. The same steps repeated for the second condition, followed by the second IPQ. Playthroughs ran without a time limit: participants took as long as they wanted, and the game does not punish hesitation. We disinfected the headset after each session.

Between conditions the experimenters added or removed the physical proxies, depending on the assigned order. One researcher operated the water spray for the pipe challenge in the haptic condition. Timing it required no screen mirroring: the virtual burst triggers when the coin leaves the collider in front of the pipe, so the researcher watched the participant's hand and started spraying the moment the physical coin left the physical pipe.
