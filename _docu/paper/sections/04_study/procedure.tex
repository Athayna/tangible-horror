\subsection{Procedure}
\label{sec:study-procedure}

Sessions took place in a work room at Hochschule RheinMain that was prepared as the play area, with the proxy stations set up and calibrated before participants arrived. Participants were told beforehand that they would be taking part in a study on VR horror and gave verbal consent; no written consent forms were used, which we return to in the limitations. After a short briefing on the headset, the controls, and the horror content---participants knew the genre but not the individual challenges---each participant put on the Quest~3 and played their first condition until they escaped the cabin. They then took the headset off and filled in the IPQ. The same steps were repeated for the second condition, followed by the second IPQ. Playthroughs were untimed: participants could take as long as they wanted, and nothing in the game punishes hesitation. The headset was disinfected after every session.

Between conditions the experimenters added or removed the physical proxies, depending on the assigned order. One researcher operated the water spray for the pipe challenge in the haptic condition. No screen mirroring was needed to time it: since the virtual burst triggers when the coin leaves the collider directly in front of the pipe, the researcher could simply watch the participant's hand and start spraying the moment the physical coin came out of the physical pipe.
