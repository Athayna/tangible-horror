\subsection{Measures}
\label{sec:study-measures}

We measured presence with the IPQ~\cite{Schubert.2001, Schubert.2003, igroup}, completed once after each condition. The IPQ consists of 14 items on 7-point scales and yields a general presence item (``the sense of being there'') plus three subscales: spatial presence, involvement, and experienced realism. Three of our four challenges are player-initiated and should therefore act on place illusion. The bursting pipe, triggered and timed by an experimenter, is the only one that fits the plausibility side, so our manipulation is predominantly, though not purely, a place-illusion manipulation in Slater's sense. We chose the IPQ over alternatives on two grounds. 
First, its subscales separate the aspects a haptic proxy should move: if the proxy works by adding a valid sensorimotor action, the gain should concentrate on spatial presence and involvement rather than on experienced realism. Second, in a systematic comparison of four presence questionnaires across five isolated presence dimensions, the IPQ reached its own greatest sensitivity in the place-illusion scenario, and its score differences were the ones participants agreed with most often~\cite{presence-questionnaires}. For a manipulation of that kind the IPQ is therefore the appropriate instrument rather than a merely convenient one.
