\section{Study}
\label{sec:study}

We compared two conditions in a within-subject design. In the \emph{haptic} condition, all four physical proxies were in place and aligned with their virtual counterparts; in the \emph{baseline} condition, participants played the identical scene with no physical objects in the play area. Participants played both conditions in a single session, completing the full cabin escape each time. We randomized the order of conditions per participant to counter order effects; of the five participants who completed both conditions, three started with the haptic version and two with the baseline.
The condition comparison is coarse by design: proxies are either all present or all absent. A per-challenge manipulation would have told us which proxy carries the effect, at the cost of a much larger design; with a single playthrough per condition we judged the all-or-nothing contrast to be the honest scope for a project of this size.
